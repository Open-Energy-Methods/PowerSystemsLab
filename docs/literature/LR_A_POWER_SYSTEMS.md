# LR-A — Power systems engineering: critical foundations

## 1. Steady-state network physics
The power-flow problem is nonlinear in complex bus voltages. A suitable independent implementation begins with I=Ybus V and S_i=V_i conj(I_i), then separates slack, PV and PQ constraints. Newton–Raphson solves mismatch equations by local linearisation. National Academies [R03] identifies sparse Newton methods as central to modern analysis. This does not establish convergence for every operating point: weak grids, controller limits and a near-singular Jacobian require additional diagnostics. A converged solution may be an infeasible engineering operating point.

Thurner et al. [R01] describe an element-based Python approach to balanced static and quasi-static studies and the historical PYPOWER ancestry. Zimmerman et al. [R02] describe extensible steady-state and optimal-power-flow tooling. These sources support using both packages, but comparing two related solver implementations is not as strong as comparison to an independently derived solution. Our two-bus closed form is a good initial check. Extend with separately coded admittance assembly, transformer taps and phase shift, PV reactive limits, disconnected islands, poor initial conditions, condition number, continuation or load scaling. Preserve solver mismatches and distinguish numerical feasibility from voltage/thermal limits. CIGRE [R04] provides a reference-network concept, but original brochure data have not been acquired; native packaged cases are regression checks. [R26] supplies a later quasi-static time-series lead.

## 2. Electrical equipment and electrothermal ratings
Equipment impedance and thermal rating are not interchangeable. Transformer series impedance from rated short-circuit voltage describes a positive-sequence model on a stated power/voltage base; vector group and neutral connections determine zero-sequence pathways. A winding connection alone cannot identify the magnitude of zero-sequence impedance. Utility test guidance [R15] is a lead to actual measured data, not a universal connection requirement. Candidate YNd11/Dyn11 and separate MV earthing transformers remain conceptual. Create positive, negative and zero-sequence diagrams independently, specify neutral resistor and grounding-transformer impedance, check open/closed ties and multiple neutral paths. Do not extend the balanced builder directly to earth faults.

IEC cable rating scope [R13] confirms that steady-state ampacity is an electrothermal problem and includes differing installation arrangements. Candidate cable resistance and 200 A rating in the campus are placeholders. Obtain conductor section/material, screen and bonding, installation, ambient/soil thermal resistance, grouping, temperature limit and losses. Build a thermal-network model only after a matched reference example. IEC transformer loading scope [R14] concerns mineral-oil-immersed equipment; do not apply it to a dry-type transformer without matching guidance. For both equipment types, use measured or manufacturer losses and cooling conditions rather than a nameplate loading percentage as a complete thermal assessment.

## 3. Fault physics and protection
The current IEC edition is R05 (2026), whereas our pinned solver cites R06 (2016) [R07]. Preserve the current computational baseline until an authorized edition comparison determines changed methods and national adoption. Initial symmetrical RMS current, asymmetric peak, breaking and equivalent thermal duty are distinct. Schneider's legacy radial calculations [R08] provide a useful independent exercise, but not a current compliance reference. Max/min faults require different source, voltage and conductor-temperature assumptions. Ground-fault calculations require sequence-network data; device duty must be evaluated through the device, not simply at the bus.

The IEC relay catalogue [R09] establishes functional scope, not detailed reset algorithms. The educational integrating relay is a meaningful mathematical experiment with an instantaneous reset assumption. Add finite reset, filtering, measurement tolerance, CT saturation and breaker operating time only with documented model choices. Device coordination is a constrained feasibility problem spanning maximum load, minimum fault, inrush, withstand and grading. IEC LV breaker scope [R10] and the vendor coordination guide [R11] justify separating rated breaking, making and withstand capacities from tested selectivity/cascading combinations. Inverse-time curves alone do not establish all high-current selectivity.

## 4. Machines, dynamics and model domains
Hatziargyriou et al. [R16] motivate the extended stability taxonomy because of fast converter behaviour. A steady-state solution does not establish rotor-angle, frequency, voltage, resonance or converter-driven stability. Use operating-point initialization, differential-algebraic models, equilibrium/residual checks, and step-size or solver convergence. For the generator swing equation use consistent MVA base and mechanical speed/power conventions. Rotor inertia alone is not total engine-generator inertia. [R12] contains alternator-only shaft inertia and electrical data: a useful mechanical–electrical bridge, but requires engine and coupling data for a complete standby model.

Cui et al. [R17] present symbolic/numeric DAE modelling and compare ANDES cases to a commercial solver. Their reported comparisons support a candidate independent dynamic benchmark strategy, not automatic validity for our campus. Start with a single-machine swing equation analytical limit; then introduce governor/AVR blocks and qualified dynamic data; compare matched states, controls, events and base conventions. Generic governor and excitation studies can be labelled sensitivity studies; do not imply a particular genset's response.

## 5. Electromagnetic transients and power electronics
RMS studies approximate fundamental-frequency phasors; EMT solves time-domain circuit behaviour. PSCAD's residual-flux example [R18] provides a route to a benchmark, not a verified campus model. Transformer energisation depends on flux initial conditions, nonlinear magnetisation, source strength, breaker timing, and numerical step. The free edition boundary [R19] motivates isolated test circuits. Independently verify an R-L fault waveform and peak before saturation, residual flux, three-phase energisation or UPS front-end models. Report time-step sensitivity and initialization. A rectifier or averaged inverter model cannot establish harmonic emissions or grid-forming performance without control and switching detail.

## 6. Grid integration, operations and digital systems
R02 gives an OPF starting point. Progress from feasibility to constrained dispatch, active/reactive limits, contingency constraints, sensitivity and reproducible objective/residual reporting. R20 and R24 show why large computational loads need grid-facing behaviour models beyond constant PQ. This later branch should cover voltage-dependent loads, frequency response, converter current limitation, recovery and aggregate interconnection behaviour. R26 is a time-series benchmark lead. Grid connection rules are jurisdiction- and operator-specific; EU/German or Swedish requirements need their own verified register. IEC 61850/CIM, state estimation, HVDC/FACTS, harmonics, earthing/arc flash and reliability methods remain full-text research streams, not completed review domains.

## 7. Research agenda and limitations
Immediate competence gates: independently derive network equations; reproduce CT158 under its historical assumptions; add transformer and earth-return sequence networks; implement relay reset variants; source equipment data. Later topics: voltage sensitivity and tap control, machine/AVR dynamics, converter controls and interconnection, optimization, measurement/uncertainty and low-energy laboratory validation. No textbooks were retrieved in full during this pass; the main reading list remains a candidate list. Literature on advanced protection, HVDC, harmonics, probabilistic reliability and hardware experiments needs dedicated searches and primary evidence. All conclusions here are a targeted synthesis and project recommendations, not a comprehensive state of the art.
